What is a foreclosure surplus?

When a home is sold in foreclosure, the lender is owed a set amount — the unpaid mortgage balance plus fees. If the property sells for more than that amount, the difference is called a surplus.

That surplus doesn't go to the bank. Under New York law, it belongs to the former homeowner. But it doesn't arrive automatically — it sits with the court until someone files a claim.

Example
Foreclosure sale price$320,000
Mortgage owed$245,000
Surplus held by court$75,000

That $75,000 may belong to the former owner.
